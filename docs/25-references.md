## Acidification, Proteolysis, and Dough Rheology

- Clarke CI, Schober TJ, Dockery P, O’Sullivan K, Arendt EK (2004). Wheat sourdough fermentation: effects of time and acidification on fundamental rheological properties. *Cereal Chemistry* 81(3):409–417. — Source for the charge-state/electrostatic repulsion mechanism.
- Clarke CI, Schober TJ, Arendt EK (2002). Effect of single strain and traditional mixed strain starter cultures on rheological properties of wheat dough and on bread quality. *Cereal Chemistry* 79(5):640–647.
- Thiele C, Grassl S, Gänzle MG (2004). Gluten hydrolysis and depolymerization during sourdough fermentation. *Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry* 52(5):1307–1314. — Key source for pH-dependent proteolytic depolymerisation of glutenin.
- Wehrle K, Grau H, Arendt EK (1997). Effects of lactic acid, acetic acid, and table salt on fundamental rheological properties of wheat dough. *Cereal Chemistry* 74(6):739–744.
- **Note on contested evidence:** chemical acidification (direct acid addition) and biological acidification (sourdough fermentation) do not produce equivalent rheology. Several studies report that direct acid addition *firms* dough and *reduces* extensibility, while extended biological fermentation — which combines acidification with time-dependent proteolysis — softens it. This document’s account of increased extensibility refers to the biological case.

- **Note:** the Q₁₀ ≈ 2 approximation for enzyme rate versus temperature is a general biochemical relationship rather than a bread-specific finding; it is used here as an order-of-magnitude guide, not a precise predictor for any particular protease isoform.

## Thermal Death Kinetics and Stress Recovery

- Leao C, van Uden N (1982). Effects of ethanol and other alkanols on the kinetics and the activation parameters of thermal death in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. *Biotechnology and Bioengineering* 24:1581-1590.
- Piper PW (1995). The heat shock and ethanol stress responses of yeast exhibit extensive similarity and functional overlap. *FEMS Microbiology Letters* 134(2-3):121-127.
- Piper PW et al. (1994). Induction of major heat-shock proteins of *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, including plasma membrane Hsp30, by ethanol levels above a critical threshold. *Microbiology* 140(11):3031-3038.
- Aguilera A, Benitez T (1989). Synergistic effects of ethanol and temperature on yeast mitochondria. *Current Microbiology* 18:179-188.
- Guyot S, Ferret E, Gervais P. Thermal death rate of *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*: dependence on the kinetics of heat perturbation. - Slow ramps confer greater resistance than sudden shock.
- Guerrero S, Lopez-Malo A, Alzamora SM. *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* thermal inactivation kinetics combined with ultrasound. - First-order kinetics, D-values 22.3-0.8 min at 45-55 C.
- Shui W et al. A hierarchical transcriptional regulatory network required for long-term thermal stress tolerance in an industrial *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* strain. - Short-term vs long-term thermotolerance are regulated differently.

> **Caveat:** published D- and z-values are measured in buffers or growth media, not dough. Dough solutes are broadly protective, so literature values are lower bounds on survival time. Use the kinetics qualitatively.
