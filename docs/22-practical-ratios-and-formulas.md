# Practical Ratios and Formulas

The following ratios are starting points for a home oven pizza bake at 250–300°C. The key variable is the levain percentage (as a proportion of total flour weight), which controls acid contribution without interfering with the commercial yeast’s leavening role.

| Parameter | Conservative | Moderate | Bold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Levain (% of total flour) | 10% | 15% | 20% |
| Commercial yeast (IDY) | 0.3% of total flour | 0.2% of total flour | 0.15% of total flour |
| Levain hydration | 100% (liquid) | 100% (liquid) | 60% (stiff) |
| Levain maturity | Young, just peaked | Just peaked | Peaked, slight decline OK |
| Acid character | Subtle background tang | Noticeable lactic roundness | Distinct, complex tang |
| Gluten impact | Minimal | Slight softening | Moderate — may need stronger flour |
| Flavour in home oven | Mild enhancement | Clear sourdough character | Pronounced, rustic |

## Levain Type: Liquid vs. Stiff for the Hybrid

For the conservative and moderate ratios, a liquid levain (100% hydration) is preferred. It disperses easily into the final mix, provides primarily lactic acid (milder, rounder), and integrates without creating dry spots or uneven hydration. At the bold 20% ratio, a stiff levain (60% hydration) becomes an option — the acetic acid contribution adds a sharper edge that can stand up to aggressive toppings and the longer bake of a home oven. When using a stiff levain, adjust the final dough water to account for the lower water contribution from the preferment.

## Inverse Relationship: Levain Up, Yeast Down

Note the inverse relationship between levain percentage and commercial yeast dose. As the levain contribution increases, it brings its own wild yeast population, which supplements leavening. Reducing the commercial yeast dose compensates for this, keeping total gas production consistent and preventing over-proofing. At 20% levain with a mature culture, you may find that 0.1% IDY is sufficient — the wild yeast is doing meaningful leavening work at that point.

## Sample Formula: Home Oven (275–300°C) — 4 Balls (~250g each)

This is the moderate ratio (15% levain) formulated for a standard home oven, targeting approximately 65% total hydration with a Tipo 00 or strong bread flour (W280–320).

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker’s % |
|---|---|---|
| Flour (Tipo 00 or strong bread flour) | 550g | 100% |
| Water | 340g | ~62% |
| Liquid levain (100% hydration, at peak) | 165g | 15% flour equivalent |
| Fine sea salt | 16g | 2.9% |
| Instant dry yeast | 1.1g | 0.2% |
| Olive oil (optional) | 15g | 2.7% |

**Note on hydration calculation:** The 165g of liquid levain at 100% hydration contributes approximately 82g flour and 82g water. Total flour is therefore ~632g (550 + 82), total water ~422g (340 + 82), giving a true hydration of ~67%. The water in the formula is adjusted downward to account for the levain’s contribution.

## Sample Formula: Ooni 12” (450°C+) — 4 Balls (~275g each)

This formula is optimised for a 12” Ooni with a 48h cold retard. Key differences from the home oven version: lower yeast dose (the dough must survive 48h without over-proofing), slightly lower hydration for peel handling, larger ball weight for thermal mass and turning clearance, and the long retard is mandatory for sugar depletion.

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker’s % |
|---|---|---|
| Flour (Tipo 00, ideally W280–320) | 600g | 100% |
| Water | 350g | ~58% |
| Liquid levain (100% hydration, at peak) | 150g | ~12.5% flour equivalent |
| Fine sea salt | 18g | 2.7% |
| Instant dry yeast | 0.4g | 0.06% |
| Olive oil (optional) | 12g | 1.8% |

**Note on hydration:** The 150g of liquid levain contributes ~75g flour and ~75g water. Total flour is ~675g, total water ~425g, giving a true hydration of ~63%. This sits at the lower end of the Ooni-friendly window for clean peel handling.

**Note on yeast dose:** At 0.06% IDY, the commercial yeast provides a slow, steady leavening contribution that, combined with the wild yeast from the levain, produces adequate aeration over a 48–72h cold retard without over-proofing. If the dough feels under-proofed at 48h, increase to 0.1% on the next batch. If over-proofed or blown at 48h, reduce further or extend the cold retard.

**Note on sugar depletion:** The 48h minimum cold retard is non-negotiable for Ooni temperatures. At 48h, the yeast has consumed most available simple sugars, yielding controlled leopard-spot charring rather than uniform burning. At 24h, sugar depletion may be insufficient for a burner at full output.

### Method (Both Formulas)

**1. Build the levain (8–12h before mixing):** Feed your sourdough culture at 1:5:5 (culture:flour:water) with white bread flour. Ferment at room temperature until just peaked — domed, bubbly, passes the float test. For maximum yeast vigour relative to LAB population, catch it just at or slightly before peak.

**2. Autolyse (30–60 min):** Combine the flour with most of the water (reserve ~30g). Mix until no dry flour remains, cover, and rest. This hydrates the flour and begins gluten development before the acid from the levain is introduced.

**3. Final mix:** Add the levain, remaining water (with the yeast dissolved in it), salt, and olive oil to the autolysed dough. Mix by hand or stand mixer until well incorporated and moderately developed — smooth, slightly tacky, passes a windowpane test with moderate translucency.

**4. Bulk ferment (2–3h at ~22°C):** Perform 3–4 stretch-and-fold sets at 30-minute intervals during the first 90 minutes, then leave undisturbed. The dough should increase in volume by roughly 50–70%. The commercial yeast provides predictable timing here.

**5. Ball and cold retard:** Divide into balls (250g for home oven; for Ooni, 270–280g for a thicker forgiving crust or 220–230g for a thinner crisp style), round tightly, and place in individual oiled containers. Refrigerate at 3–5°C. **Home oven:** 24–72h. **Ooni:** 48–72h minimum — the extended retard is essential for sugar depletion at high bake temperatures. During the retard, LAB continue producing acid at a reduced rate (building flavour), yeast activity slows to near zero, and the ester precursor pool accumulates.

**6. Temper with gentle pull (1.5–2h at room temperature):** Remove balls from the fridge. Before setting them aside to temper, give each ball a gentle stretch-and-fold: lift one edge, stretch it outward without tearing, fold it back over the centre, rotate 90°, and repeat 2–3 times. Re-round loosely without degassing. This gentle mechanical input creates nucleation sites for supersaturated dissolved CO₂ (Henry’s law — more gas dissolves at fridge temperatures than the dough can hold at room temperature) and realigns the gluten network for better gas retention. The dough will show visible, rapid expansion within 20–30 minutes as dissolved gas nucleates into bubbles, followed by continued rise as the yeast reactivates. The dough is ready when relaxed, puffy, and extensible. Ooni doughs at very low yeast doses may need slightly longer tempering.

**7. Bake: Home oven (250–300°C, 6–10 min):** Open by hand, top, and bake on a preheated stone or steel. The longer bake time gives the thermal esterification reactions more time to develop — the crust develops a rich, complex aromatic profile from the converging alcohol and acid precursor pools. **Ooni 12” (350–380°C stone):** Preheat at full flame for ~15 minutes, then drop to 40–50% before launch. For larger balls (270–280g), open to 10–11” and bake 2–3 minutes with turns every 20–30 seconds. For smaller balls (220–230g), open to 11–12” and bake 90s–2 minutes. Optionally boost the flame briefly in the final 15–20 seconds for leopard-spot char. Allow 3–5 minutes of stone recovery at full flame between pizzas.
