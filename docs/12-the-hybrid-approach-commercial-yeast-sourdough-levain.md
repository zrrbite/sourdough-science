# The Hybrid Approach: Commercial Yeast + Sourdough Levain

The most compelling strategy for home pizza may be a hybrid: use commercial *S. cerevisiae* as the primary leavening agent for reliable gas production and schedule predictability, and incorporate a small proportion of sourdough levain purely as a flavour and acid substrate. This decouples the two functions that a sourdough culture normally performs simultaneously — leavening and flavour development — and optimises each independently.

## Why Decouple Leavening from Flavour?

In a 100% sourdough pizza dough, the wild yeast must do all the leavening work. This means the dough needs extended fermentation time, which exposes the gluten network to prolonged proteolytic degradation from LAB enzymes. The result is often a dough that is fragile, tear-prone during stretching, and difficult to time precisely. By handing gas production to commercial yeast, total fermentation time shortens dramatically, reducing protease exposure. The gluten network stays more intact, giving better stretch and structural resilience during hand opening. Meanwhile, the levain contribution — even at a small percentage — provides the organic acid substrate needed for flavour complexity.
