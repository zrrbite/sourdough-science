# Application: Pizza Dough

Pizza dough sits at the intersection of all four preferment quadrants, and strong traditions exist behind each approach. The choice of preferment has direct consequences for dough handling, flavour, and oven performance.

## The Neapolitan Approach

The Neapolitan tradition favours poolish or direct commercial yeast with a long cold ferment (24–72 hours). The goal is maximum extensibility without acid interference. The dough should stretch thin without tearing, blister aggressively in a hot oven, and have a sweet, wheaty, slightly yeasty flavour. Acetic acid works against this goal because it tightens the dough, and excessive proteolytic degradation from a long sourdough ferment can make it fragile and tear-prone during hand stretching.

## Sourdough Pizza

Sourdough pizza is entirely viable but requires managing the acid load. A young, liquid levain at peak activity (maximum yeast vigour relative to LAB population) provides the gas production needed for cornicione puff without excessive acidification. Some bakers use a stiff levain specifically because the slower fermentation gives a wider timing window — the dough is more forgiving if you cannot get to it at exactly the right moment.

### Practical Adjustments for Sourdough Pizza

The acid-modified gluten in a sourdough pizza dough is more extensible but less elastic. This often requires reducing hydration slightly compared to a commercial yeast formula, or increasing flour protein content to compensate. The stretch will feel different: less snap-back, more willingness to thin out, but also less structural resilience if overworked.

## Oven Temperature and Preferment Choice

Oven temperature interacts with preferment choice in a meaningful way. In a proper Neapolitan deck oven at 450°C+, the bake is so fast (60–90 seconds) that subtle flavour differences compress and char dominates. At home oven temperatures (250–300°C), the longer bake time gives the crust more time to develop, and the acid complexity of a sourdough preferment shines through. For home baking, sourdough pizza can actually be the more flavourful option precisely because the slower bake allows its distinctive character to express fully.
