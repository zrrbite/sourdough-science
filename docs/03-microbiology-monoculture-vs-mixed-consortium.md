# Microbiology: Monoculture vs. Mixed Consortium

## Commercial Yeast Preferments (Biga and Poolish)

Both biga and poolish are inoculated with a pure culture of *S. cerevisiae*. This is a domesticated, single-strain monoculture. The long, cool fermentation period is not intended to cultivate a diverse microbiome; rather, it allows the yeast to produce flavour precursors through slow metabolic activity. Alcohols, esters, and aldehydes accumulate over 12–24 hours, contributing nuanced aroma without significant acidification. Some minor organic acid production occurs from the yeast’s own metabolism, but nothing approaching the lactic and acetic acid concentrations generated by LAB.

## Sourdough Preferments (Stiff and Liquid Levain)

A sourdough levain is a mixed-culture system comprising wild yeast (most often wild *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* in home starters, *Kazachstania humilis* in many bakery ones) and LAB (mostly lactobacilli such as *L. plantarum*, *L. brevis* and *Fructilactobacillus sanfranciscensis*; *Leuconostoc* belongs to the first days of a new starter, not the settled culture) in a stable association. How far it is a true symbiosis is debated: the bacteria gain from the yeast more than the yeast gains from them. *The finished culture* in [Starting a Starter From Scratch](../notes/starter-from-scratch.md) has the evidence. The LAB produce lactic and acetic acid, dropping pH into the 3.5–4.5 range. The wild yeast strains are adapted to thrive in this acidic environment, which suppresses competing organisms and creates a stable, self-sustaining culture.

This mixed ecology has several consequences that monoculture yeast preferments cannot replicate. Proteolytic enzymes from the LAB partially degrade the gluten network during fermentation. Gas production is less uniform because multiple organisms with different growth curves and temperature optima are competing and cooperating simultaneously. And the acid load directly modifies gluten charge state and hydration behaviour.
