# Starter Diary

What the jars in this kitchen actually did, feed by feed, newest run first. The method and
the reasoning behind each run live in [Starting a Starter From Scratch](starter-from-scratch.md);
this file holds the readings. The columns follow the video's *Day by day* table there, so a
run can be read against it day for day.

Rise is the peak height against a rubber band at the level of the fresh feed. Feeds are
starter / water / flour in grams.

---

## Flakes in a new start, November 2026

**Setup.** A new day-1 mix with a pinch of crushed dried starter in it, not weighed. On a
desk measured at 23.5°C by day, under muslin. Day 1 was 6 November. From
8 November the flour is the 30% rye mix: 350 g plain flour and 150 g dark wholemeal rye.
Fed every 12 hours at 1:1:1, once a day during a lull (the run's [feeding
schedule](starter-from-scratch.md#flakes-in-a-new-start-own-run-november-2026-in-progress)).

**Reading so far** *(hypothesis)*. The first 2× came on day 4, the day the video's first
wave peaks (3–4× on day 4, with unpleasant smells). Here the smell was pleasant and slightly
acidic, which fits the flakes' sourdough bacteria rather than the flour's first wave, though
the smell may not separate them in this kitchen. Yeast this early would be unusual (*Why the
yeast comes last*). The next two days decide: a flat, runny day means it was the first wave;
steady activity means the flakes skipped it.

Day 5, the video's lull day, brought no lull: all three jars rose to almost 2× within about
9 hours with an acidic smell, and the jar fed 1:2:2 kept pace with the 1:1:1 jar. So far
that fits the flakes having skipped the first wave. Day 6 is the last chance for a late
lull.
